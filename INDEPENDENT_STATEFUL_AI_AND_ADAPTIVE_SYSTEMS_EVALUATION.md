## Purpose

Collapse Aware AI was developed around a narrow engineering question: how can retained prior state influence future behavioural selection in a controlled, measurable and inspectable way?

That work also creates a separate service capability that does **not** require a customer to license or integrate CAAI middleware.

Inappropriate Media Limited can undertake bounded technical evaluations of **stateful, adaptive and history-sensitive AI systems**, including agents, workflows, simulations, training systems, game/NPC systems and other software in which prior interaction may alter later behaviour.

The aim is practical:

> **Given a system that changes over time, determine what retained history, perturbation or intervention actually changes what the system does next — and preserve evidence of the result.**

This is an independent evaluation and analysis service. It is not a claim that every adaptive system follows Verrell's Law, and it does not require acceptance of any speculative physical interpretation.

## 1. History-Sensitivity / Retained-State Audit

A customer provides one bounded agent, workflow, simulator or decision process.

Where technically possible, matched present conditions are constructed after different prior histories:

```text
History A ─┐
History B ─┼─> matched present condition ─> same subsequent test
History C ─┘
```

The evaluation asks questions such as:

- Does different retained history alter later behaviour under otherwise matched present conditions?
- Which prior events materially influence the result?
- Is the effect stable across repeated runs?
- Does apparently irrelevant history contaminate later decisions?
- Does removing, revising or replacing retained state remove the observed divergence?
- Can a previous trajectory or decision be reproduced under controlled conditions?

Possible outputs include a test protocol, controlled-history cases, baseline comparisons, divergence analysis, replay evidence and a concise technical report.

---

## 2. Emergent Behaviour Stress Test

For adaptive or multi-step systems, behaviour can change when memory, ordering, tools, feedback, environment or other operating conditions are perturbed.

A bounded stress test can vary declared factors such as:

- retained state or memory availability;
- event ordering;
- prior outcomes;
- tool availability;
- agent population or role configuration;
- environmental conditions;
- human intervention points;
- feedback and correction history;
- latency or execution conditions where relevant.

The objective is not to label behaviour as "emergent" merely because it is surprising.

The objective is to identify:

> **the conditions under which behaviour departs materially from the declared baseline, whether that departure is reproducible, and what evidence supports the finding.**

---

## 3. Stateful Agent / Cognitive Infrastructure Review

Some organisations already have memory, retrieval, tool use, feedback, state and orchestration components but do not have a clear map of how those components influence behaviour over time.

A review can examine areas such as:

- what state persists;
- what state is merely retrieved;
- what state can actually influence action;
- how user corrections and contradictory information are handled;
- whether stale state can contaminate later behaviour;
- how salience, recency and repetition interact;
- how open tasks or commitments are preserved;
- what can be replayed or inspected after a decision;
- where hidden state can create behavioural divergence.

The deliverable is an architecture/evidence review rather than a middleware installation.

---

## 4. Intervention-Sensitivity / Human-in-the-Loop Analysis

Earlier CAAI research sometimes used the phrase **observer-linked simulation**. For practical evaluation, the narrower and more useful question is intervention sensitivity:

> Does changing when, how or whether an operator, evaluator or external system intervenes alter the later trajectory?

This can be relevant to supervised agents, training simulations, moderation systems, fraud/investigation workflows and other human-in-the-loop environments.

The test boundary, permitted interventions and measurable outcomes are declared before the evaluation.

## What a customer can bring

A suitable first engagement does not need to be large.

A customer can bring:

1. one real or anonymised system/problem;
2. the behaviour or decision they want investigated;
3. relevant state/history that may matter;
4. permitted test conditions or perturbations;
5. access to outputs, logs or an evaluation interface sufficient to observe results.

Where direct system access is not appropriate, a customer may instead provide a bounded simulation, test harness, anonymised traces or reproducible scenario.

---

## What we can return

Depending on scope, an engagement can return:

- a predeclared evaluation protocol;
- matched-history or perturbation test cases;
- baseline-versus-variant comparisons;
- divergence/stability/replay results;
- evidence tables or Decision Record-style summaries;
- failure or ambiguity notes;
- an architecture assessment;
- recommendations for further testing or engineering controls;
- a customer-safe PDF or structured evidence pack.

Results are reported within the tested regime. A negative or null result remains a valid result.

## Commercial boundary

This service is deliberately separate from mandatory CAAI software adoption.

A customer may commission an independent evaluation without licensing CAAI Core Gold.

If an evaluation identifies a problem that CAAI Core Gold may address, a separate software evaluation or licensing discussion can follow. There is no requirement to do so.

**Core Gold** is the finished, frozen CAAI product currently available for commercial evaluation/licensing discussions.

**Evolution 2** remains an engineering programme and is **not** being offered through this service as a finished commercial product.

---

## Scientific and claim boundary

The present commercial service is strongest for **synthetic/software systems** where histories, interventions and outcomes can be controlled or observed.

Biological/synthetic parallels remain a research and potential collaboration area. They should not be represented as a currently validated commercial capability without appropriate domain partners, data and experimental controls.

Likewise, these services do not claim to establish consciousness, quantum collapse, a universal physical memory field or a universal law of nature.

---

## Relationship to Verrell's Law

Verrell's Law provides one research framework for asking whether retained informational history contributes structured, directional and intervention-sensitive changes in later selection.

The independent evaluation service uses the same discipline where useful — matched conditions, explicit history differences, perturbation, ablation, repetition and falsifiable reporting — without assuming in advance that the hypothesis must be supported.
